Where the data comes from, what it covers and what to consider when reading the charts.

Data sources

This project uses cleaned extracts of the national, state and territory, and First Nations hospitalised road injury datasets published through the Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE) and the Road Safety Data Hub. The underlying hospital data is sourced from the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW).

The BITRE Hospitalised Injury publication provides the three workbooks. The September 2023 data dictionary (PDF) explains their definitions and reporting notes.

This link provides information about the ABS Remoteness Areas in what categories they are and how the areas are categorized.

Data coverage

The supplied datasets cover Australian road traffic injuries from 2011 to 2021. They describe hospital records, rather than every road crash or injury in the community.

Understanding the measures

  • Overall cases: the national dataset's total case count, including recorded deaths in care.
  • Hospitalisations: the national chart files use total cases minus recorded deaths. Other charts use the hospitalisation counts supplied in their cleaned files.
  • Recorded deaths in care: the source defines this as deaths during hospital care within 30 days of admission. This measure is not a count of every road death.
  • Bed days: recorded days of hospital care, a separate measure from the number of cases.

Limitations

  • Counts and risk: population sizes differ between groups. Counts and shares of hospitalisations do not establish injury risk per person.
  • Partial 2014 records: the cleaned First Nations dataset is missing July–December entries for ages 0–7 and 65+ in both populations. Its 2014 bars use the available records and represent a partial comparison.
  • Unknown categories: the male/female age comparison excludes 16 hospitalisations with unknown age or sex. The overall cases and mortality strip includes all available categories.
  • Reporting changes: Victoria's series changes from 2012, and NSW's from June 2017. Comparisons across these breaks require care, as described in the source data dictionary.
  • Different age bands: the First Nations file groups older ages as 65+, while the national file separates 65–74 and 75+. These categories cannot be compared directly without regrouping.
  • Vague labelling: the methodology used to measure the remoteness of an area is not clearly defined and needed an external website source to clarify which area classified as which.

The website uses grouped counts without names or contact details. Group comparisons should be interpreted respectfully and in context; the charts do not explain why differences occur.
